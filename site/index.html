Why it sticks

Built for the ten minutes you'll actually give it.

Stuff you'll actually use

Train stations, konbini, restaurants, pharmacies. Nothing is here because a textbook chapter needed filling.

It knows what you're forgetting

Each session is assembled from whatever is closest to slipping away, so ten minutes land where they count instead of on the things you already know.

A readiness score, not a streak

One honest number for how close you are to handling a trip unaided, weighted by what travel actually demands. Miss a day and it tells you the truth rather than punishing you.

Say it out loud

Speaking drills listen to you through the phone's own microphone and tell you whether what you said was what you meant. There is a silent mode for the train.

Watch it played out

Whole exchanges — the ticket window, the counter, the convenience store — run start to finish, in kana, once the alphabet is solid enough to read them.

What nobody tells you

Notes on the things that are not vocabulary: which queue to join, why nobody answers a phone on the train, what a shopkeeper's silence means.

A session, end to end

Ten minutes, three moves.

STEP 1

Open it

The lesson is built the moment you start it — from where you are today, not from a plan drawn up last week.

STEP 2

Clear what's fading

Whatever is closest to slipping goes first. When there is room left, one new thing is introduced, and not before.

STEP 3

Reveal, then be honest

Again or Got it. That one answer decides when the card comes back — days away if it landed, minutes if it didn't.
